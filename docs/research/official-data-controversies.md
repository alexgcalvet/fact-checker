## Conclusion

An official figure is not the truth by itself. Three kinds of problem:

| Kind | Example | What the tool must do |
|---|---|---|
| Definition in dispute | Registered unemployment leaves out inactive fijos discontinuos ("demandantes con relación laboral", about 880,000–900,000 at the end of 2025). Estimates of "real" unemployment run from 3.3 to 3.9 million; the EPA (ILO definition) is about 2.6 million | Show each credible measure; a claim that one credible measure supports is not "false" |
| Framing in dispute | Immigration and crime: counts or rates, nationality or origin, irregular migrants missing from the figures | Name the cut the claim uses; never pick one side's cut as the truth |
| Producer under political control | The CIS: led by a party member, documented method criticism | Lower confidence; prefer independent producers |

## Source tiers

| Tier | Producers | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Independent statistics with international methods: INE, Eurostat, Banco de España, OECD | Full weight |
| 2 | Administrative records of a ministry: SEPE, Interior, Seguridad Social | Full weight, but check for a documented controversy on the metric |
| 3 | Government communication: La Moncloa, ministry press releases | Lower confidence |
| 4 | Producers under political control: CIS | Lower confidence; never the only source |
| 5 | Think tanks and media of any side | Context only; never the only source for "false" |

## Rule for flags

- "false": every credible measure contradicts the claim.
- "disputed": one credible measure supports it and another does not. The card shows each measure with its source.
- A documented controversy on the metric is shown on the card, whatever the verdict.
